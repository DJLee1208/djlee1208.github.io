About

Learning systems that respect the decisions beneath them.

My research sits at the intersection of mathematical optimization and machine learning. I am interested in models that do more than predict: they must produce decisions that remain fast, feasible, and useful under operational constraints.

At Georgia Tech, I study scalable learning approaches for structured optimization, with particular interest in large mixed-integer systems, solver-integrated learning, and multistage decisions under uncertainty.

Research

Core directions

Methods for turning predictive signals into reliable decisions at operational scale.

01

Learning for Large-Scale Optimization

Neural proxies and learned decision policies for optimization problems where conventional solve times limit real-time use.

02

Constrained Sequential Decisions

Context-aware policies for multistage problems that couple uncertainty, recourse, and hard feasibility requirements.

03

Decision-Focused Machine Learning

Training objectives and architectures shaped by downstream decision quality rather than prediction error alone.

04

Reliable Time-Series Learning

Representation learning for forecasting and anomaly detection across long horizons and multivariate systems.

Full ML Proxies for Very Large-Scale Mixed-Integer Optimization

Developing a full machine-learning proxy for very large-scale mixed-integer optimization with temporal and fleet-wide constraints. Target instances contain more than 100,000 discrete variables; the research aims to combine ML inference with feasibility repair at millisecond-scale latency.
